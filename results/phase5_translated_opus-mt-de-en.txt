Warsaw was a multicultural city throughout its existence. According to the 1901 census, 56.2% of the population were Catholics, 35.7% Jews, 5% Greek Orthodox Christians and 2.8% Protestants. Eight years later, in 1909, there were 28,1754 Jews (36.9%), 18.189 Protestants (2.4%) and 2,818 Mariavites (0.4%). This led to the construction of hundreds of religious places of worship in all parts of the city. Most of them were destroyed after the Warsaw Uprising of 1944. After the war, the new communist authorities of Poland advised against building churches and few were rebuilt.

The basic administrative unit in Poland is called the municipality (Gmina). A city is also a municipality – but it has a city right. Both cities and municipalities are governed by a mayor – who is called Vogt (in Polish Wójt) and Burmistrz. Some larger cities receive rights, i.e. tasks and privileges belonging to the second level of administration – counties or Powiaty. One example of such a right is the registration of a car: a Gmina cannot register cars, because this is the task of the Powiat (i.e. a registration number depends on the allowed Powiat and not on the Gmina). If a city gets rights, it is called a district or Powiat Grodzki. Such cities are, for example, Lublin, Krakow, Gdansk or Poznan. In Warsaw, districts have additional rights to the Powiaty – as mentioned above. For example, the districts of Wola and Ursynów have their own registration plates (and the cars from Wola have different registration plates than those from Ursynów). However, the districts in Krakow do not have any Powiat rights. Therefore, the car registration marks in Krakow are of the same type in all districts.

The first Warsaw Stock Exchange was founded in 1817 and continued until World War II. It was restored in April 1991 after the end of the post-war communist control and the reintroduction of a free market economy. According to many indicators, the Warsaw Stock Exchange (WSE) is the largest market in the region, with 374 listed companies and a total capitalisation of 162 584 million. EUR as at 31 August 2009. From 1991 to 2000, the stock exchange was ironically located in the building, which formerly served as the headquarters of the Polish United Workers' Party (PZPR).
